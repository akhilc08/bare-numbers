# Stated units hide the sender's misreading

No published or preprint work measures unit, currency or locale mismatch between LLM agents, so L4's novelty survives as a claim about the channel. Both the literature and the project's own logs move the planned headline: the convention is lost at whichever agent first reads the bare number, and an instruction never to send a bare number makes that agent the sender. CAPRI already owns infer-but-don't-apply, and Bui et al., LocQA and CAPRI already predict the dollar default. 2026 papers on relay formats, metadata collapse, channel audits and threshold-triggered asking fill the neighbouring cells. The defensible claim is therefore convention grounding across an organisational boundary, with counterfactual attribution to sender or receiver. The headline does not contradict CAPRI. CAPRI scored accuracy under a forced two-step prompt, with no receiver and no option to ask, and explicitness raises accuracy in L4 too. Sonnet's 3% baseline comes from a resolver arm in which **80% of episodes end on a question without any action**. What the logs add is a mechanism the uncertainty literature predicts. Under the plain prompt, **37–51% of senders' first messages say the principal's unit is unknown**. The explicit instruction drives that rate to 0–2%, and the readings it forces are mostly US units that receivers trust, even over an oracle locale card in all 23 of Haiku's errors. The plausibility account has no direct precedent. It holds for senders (Sonnet: 0 of 18 silent errors on budgets implausible as dollars, against 15 of 30 on plausible ones) but not for the headline receivers, which booked every injected bare budget in dollars. Agent standards have no slot for any of this: A2A, MCP, OpenAPI and JSON Schema carry no unit, currency or locale, payment protocols type ISO 4217 only at settlement, and BCP 47 with CLDR already defines the missing locale tag. A fair ask-versus-act test needs two things: a simulated principal that both agents can reach and that answers only unit-targeted questions from CAPRI's key, and a price on every question. That rerun, a free reanalysis of the existing logs and CAPRI's own two-step sender are the most valuable cheap experiments.

## No prior paper sends a bare number across an agent boundary

Five independent literature sweeps covered tool calling, cultural defaults, clarification, agent protocols and channel forms. They converge on one gap: **no paper tests a tool or counterpart agent whose unit differs from the user's culturally implied unit while the user's number is bare.** Targeted arXiv API queries pairing locale, currency and unit with multi-agent terms returned nothing relevant, and GitHub issue searches on A2A and MCP found no adopted locale proposal ([A2A issues](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/issues); [MCP SEP-1809](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/issues/1809)). Leaderboards cannot see the failure by construction. Of BFCL v4's 400 simple items, 23 expose a unit or currency parameter. In all three items with unit-suffixed names (`weight_kg`, `temp_celsius`, `daily_miles`), the user states the same unit as the suffix ([BFCL data](https://github.com/ShishirPatil/gorilla/tree/main/berkeley-function-call-leaderboard/bfcl_eval/data)). Every benchmark scores arguments against the tool's own convention, so `mph=100` for an Iranian user counts as a correct call. Error taxonomies fold the problem into a generic value-error class that is already the dominant failure: **78.8% of Qwen2.5-72B's errors in ComplexFuncBench** and about 55% of gpt-4o's failures in τ-bench ([ComplexFuncBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10132v1); [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045v1)). That gives L4 a reusable contribution: a "convention error" subtype of ToolScan's Incorrect Argument Value ([ToolScan](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13547v2)). The closest scored construct is ToolSandbox's canonicalisation of an explicit "$" into USD, which tops out at 76.6 for GPT-4o even with the marker present ([ToolSandbox](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04682v2)).

The cleanest way to turn the two relay papers from threats into support is a **fidelity stack**. Zeng covers content fidelity: injected errors persist at 83–100% in every format. Wang et al. cover fidelity of the governing metadata. L4 covers convention grounding, the layer neither measures ([Zeng HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09678v2)). Zeng's result also does real work for L4: if strong models copy faithfully, L4's errors cannot be transmission loss. MAST supplies the failure vocabulary. The sender's omission is FM-2.4 "information withholding" (0.85% of annotated failures) and the receiver's silence is FM-2.2 "fail to ask for clarification" (6.80%), but MAST's traces contain no cross-organisation or locale cases ([MAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)). The general frame is the knowing-doing gap of Schmied et al., distributed across two agents: one knows the principal, the other acts ([Schmied et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16078)). The claim to make is the narrow one: the first controlled measurement of convention loss across organisational agent boundaries, with counterfactual attribution to sender omission versus receiver misdecoding, comparing channel designs that current protocols lack. The claims to avoid are "first to show LLMs default to USD", "first to show infer-but-don't-apply", "ask when confidence is low" as a novel receiver rule, and cost-aware clarification metrics as novel in themselves.

Timing shapes the citations. CAPRI was submitted on 16 June 2026 and had **zero citing papers on Semantic Scholar on 7 October 2026**, so L4 should say "builds on" rather than "first" ([Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2606.17688/citations)). The HTML lists nine models: eight open-weight VLMs, Gemini-3.1-Flash-Lite, and no GPT or Claude model. So the frontier-model extension is a concrete scope claim ([CAPRI HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17688v1)). The ICR audit (1 October) and the PPO-Lagrangian clarification paper (2 October) appeared days before the 12 October ARR deadline. Under ACL's roughly three-month contemporaneity convention they count as concurrent, while Matsnev (17 June) is prior work to discuss; the notes could not verify the exact rule. The theme's relevance test is that communication must be "a substantive research question, computational mechanism, or source of capability" ([NAACL 2027 CFP](https://2027.naacl.org/calls/main_conference_papers/)). L4 clears the first bar already. A read-back or two-step sender mechanism would clear the second.

## Explicitness lifts accuracy as CAPRI predicts, then silences the sender's doubt

### The 3% baseline is mostly unanswered questions

CAPRI's "explicit prompting helps" result is narrower than the headline's contrast implies. Its fix is sequential: a two-step JSON output in which the model first writes "This user is from [country]" and only then answers. That "improves VQA performance substantially, matching or exceeding the background inference task", and plain CoT helps less ([CAPRI HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17688v1)). Its other explicit condition, a user who says "I am from [country]", narrows the gap for every model except LLaMA. CAPRI scores accuracy only and has no receiver and no option to ask, so it says nothing about silent errors. On accuracy, L4 agrees with CAPRI. The table decomposes each arm in the project's logs as of 7 October 2026, computed from `results_ablation.jsonl` (Sonnet and Opus, 168 ambiguous items) and `results_frontier_haiku.jsonl` (Haiku, 84 items). Some cells are still running.

| Arm | Correct, no question | Correct after a question | Silent wrong | Wrong after a question | Ends on a question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sonnet, bare relay (33 items, partial) | 15% | 0% | 52% | 6% | 27% |
| Sonnet, receiver resolver | 15% | 0% | 3% | 2% | **80%** |
| Sonnet, sender explicit | 53% | 8% | **23%** | 0% | 15% |
| Opus, bare relay | 15% | 1% | 37% | 5% | 42% |
| Opus, receiver resolver | 18% | 3% | 3% | 8% | **68%** |
| Opus, sender explicit | 62% | 2% | 10% | 0% | 25% |
| Opus, typed with mandatory unit | 62% | 1% | 2% | 0% | 34% |
| Opus, explicit + inferred locale + resolver | 79% | 5% | 7% | 1% | 7% |
| Opus, same plus hybrid confidence | 63% | 3% | 1% | 1% | 32% |
| Haiku, oracle card + resolver | 62% | 1% | **2%** | 6% | 29% |
| Haiku, same plus sender explicit | 37% | 8% | **27%** | 1% | 26% |

Against a bare relay, explicitness raises correct outcomes and cuts silent errors (Opus 16% to 65% correct, 37% to 10% silent wrong), exactly as CAPRI would predict. The two headline contrasts mean different things. **Sonnet's 3% to 23% compares explicitness with an arm that stalls**, and any arm that rarely acts has few silent errors. **Haiku's 2% to 27% is the clean case.** With an oracle card and the resolver, adding sender explicitness lowers correct outcomes from 63% to 45% and raises silent errors from 2% to 27%, at an identical 36% question rate, so explicitness is dominated outright. For Opus, explicitness remains the largest accuracy lever, as the draft says. L4's explicit instruction ("infer your principal's locale... never a bare number") is a single free-text generation, not CAPRI's forced two-step output. The paper has therefore not yet tested CAPRI's fix in the agent setting and should not present its result as a contradiction.

### The errors are the sender's own readings, and receivers are right to trust them

Three properties of the harness locate the error. The principal's own request is bare ("only show me hotels within 5 of the conference venue"). The sender writes its first message before seeing anything from the receiver. And the tool's unit appears only in the receiver's prompt (`run.py`). A US unit in the first message is therefore the sender's reading of its own principal's number, not a copy of the counterpart's units. All 39 of Sonnet's silent errors in the explicit arm carry such a reading. Examples include "1,500 US dollars (USD)" for Chinese principals, "300 US dollars (USD)" for French ones, "100 miles per hour (mph)" for five non-US cultures, once glossed as "about 161 kilometers per hour", and "5 miles (about 8 km)" for six. All 23 of Haiku's errors are sender-written wrong units too: 22 US units for non-US principals, plus 65 km/h for a UK principal who meant mph. In the arm that also probes locale, a separate call to the same model named the principal's country correctly in **20 of 33 Sonnet silent-wrong episodes and 9 of 12 for Opus**. Most confident errors are therefore CAPRI's infer-but-don't-apply gap reproduced inside the sender's message. Overall the probe was right on 68% (Sonnet) and 71% (Opus) of items. That is below Staab et al.'s 85% top-1 location inference from Reddit text, but consistent with CAPRI's deliberately sparse cues, where background prediction reaches about 80% after one cue ([Staab et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07298); [CAPRI HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17688v1)).

A second pattern, which no prior paper reports, changes how the headline reads. **Under the plain sender prompt, senders often say they do not know the unit.** I matched patterns over first messages and spot-checked the matches by hand. Lines such as "She didn't say whether that means miles or kilometers, so I don't want to guess" appear in **51% of Sonnet's resolver-arm episodes and 37–38% of Opus's resolver and bare-relay episodes**, and 76 of Sonnet's 135 stalled resolver episodes begin this way. The explicit instruction drives the rate to **0% for Sonnet and 2% for Opus**. Many of the "receiver questions" that explicitness turns into confident actions are therefore the sender's own doubt: in other arms it is forwarded to a receiver that cannot resolve it, and in the explicit arm the prompt forbids it. The uncertainty literature predicts this. LMs rarely volunteer uncertainty, and when they do commit they are overconfident, with an average 47% error rate among confident responses ([Zhou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06730)). Preference data favours responses with fewer questions (χ² = 484.08), and more DPO training degrades grounding acts ([Shaikh et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09144v2)). A 2025 position paper argues that agents should communicate their underspecification uncertainty rather than suppress it ([Kirchhof et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22655)). The hybrid confidence channel is the remedy that line of work implies, and in the logs it lets Opus senders flag doubt again (7% of first messages).

Receivers behave as the deference literature predicts, and as a careful person would. In the project's counterfactual injections, a stated correct unit produced **no silent errors in any of the four receivers** (43–80% correct, the rest asked). That is the reviewers' NUMCoT-style control, already in hand. A stated wrong unit was followed 70–100% of the time, and a hedged one ("I'm not sure whether that is km or miles") drew a question in 68–100% of episodes. The gradient mirrors Zhou et al.'s finding that high-certainty markers lower accuracy by 7% relative to low-certainty ones while evidentials help ([Zhou et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13439)). It also mirrors the finding that models are "strongly influenced" by another model's stated prediction, slightly more so when it is framed as confident ([Anagnostidis & Bulian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11865)). Human clarification follows perceived expected regret, and moving from no uncertainty to low uncertainty changes behaviour more than moving from low to high ([Tsvilodub et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02843)). AskBench supplies the boundary condition. Gemini covers **0.749 of confidently stated false claims but only 0.124 of missing information**, because false facts can be checked against world knowledge ([AskBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11199)). A stated unit cannot be checked, and it is often legitimate: a Chinese buyer can quote a budget in dollars to a US store. That is why Haiku's receivers followed "1500 USD" over an oracle card naming China, and why the ICR audit's warning about harmful influence applies. The precise statement of the effect is that **explicitness lowers the receiver's perceived uncertainty without lowering the actual uncertainty, and removes the sender's only way to say so.** It also inverts a concurrent result. In Edwards and Schuster's coding scaffold, not asking went with easier tasks (76.92% resolved, against 65.99% when the scaffold asked). In L4, not asking on explicit messages goes with silent errors ([Edwards & Schuster](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26233)).

Prior work explains half of the US readings. CAPRI finds USD in 98% of Gemini's neutral price answers, and Bui et al. find USD in 80% of fiscal answers across seven open models. LocQA finds US answers in 50% of outputs against 26% in the data, with the bias amplified by instruction tuning, and English output itself is structurally American ([CAPRI HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17688v1); [Bui et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02591v1); [LocQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19292v1); [Nayeem & Rafiei](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04204)). CAPRI's corpus counts put speed about 70% imperial, so "100 mph" is predicted as well. But CAPRI's neutral-prompt priors are **metric for distance**, so "5 miles" for an Iranian principal is the unexplained and more novel half. Bui et al. suggest one mechanism that needs no contact with the receiver: units follow the location of the entity being discussed (73% imperial for US city distances). L4's templates never locate the hotel-booking service or rental company, and CAPRI's models default to the US as the country (Qwen-32B 94%). The explicit prompt itself also says "The other agent may be in a different country", which can cue an imagined American counterpart. One research note proposed swapping the receiver's locale to separate counterpart anchoring from a US prior. That cannot affect first messages written blind, so the test has to edit the sender's prompt or use CAPRI's neutral conversations. LocQA's taxonomy gives a coding scheme for the messages: "1,500 USD" is a Selection error, and "5 miles (about 8 km)" is a Selection error with a gloss that converts the wrong quantity.

Two caveats bound every comparison. First, request templates are fixed per concept. The 168 items are **28 culture-by-concept clusters of six conversations** (UK distance is absent and Iranian prices are excluded), per-concept contrasts rest on nine or ten clusters, and intervals must be bootstrapped over clusters. Second, Sonnet's bare-relay cell holds only 33 items, and their first messages flag the unit far less often (5 of 33) than the resolver arm's identical sender prompt does (85 of 168). That gap is too large for sampling noise, so the cell should be checked for model-alias or code drift before it serves as the reference.

### Plausibility gates the first reader, not the headline receivers

No paper found tests whether LLMs catch convention errors more readily when the misread value is implausible, so the explanation is new and has no direct precedent. The indirect evidence fits it. AskBench's asymmetry implies that a reading which contradicts world knowledge, such as a $300,000 chair, is checkable while a $300 chair is not. LMs compare quantities using "a bag of heuristics over numerals and units" rather than converting to a shared scale, which predicts magnitude-sensitive rather than unit-sensitive behaviour ([Sasaki et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03982)). A ClarifyGPT-style check, which asks when sampled interpretations disagree, would never fire on a bare "300", because every sample reads it the same way ([ClarifyGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10996)). The human record offers the closest analogue. Flight Safety Foundation notes that "altimeter setting six seven" can mean 28.67, 29.67 or 30.67 inHg or 967 hPa. In one case a QNH of 991 hPa was set as 29.91 inHg, leaving the true altitude **640 feet lower than indicated**, an error that survived because both readings are plausible ([FSF ALAR 3.1](https://flightsafety.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/alar_bn3-1-altimeter.pdf)). Mars Climate Orbiter's factor-of-4.45 error produced discrepancies that were "only informally reported" ([NASA MIB](https://llis.nasa.gov/llis_lib/pdf/1009464main1_0641-mr.pdf)). Excel's silent conversions affected about a fifth of papers with supplementary gene lists ([Ziemann et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-016-1044-7)). The counter-evidence is just as concrete: implausible values also execute when nothing forces a check. Samsung Securities issued 2.8 billion shares when 89 million existed, and employees sold 5.01 million of them ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/debriefing-the-fatfinger-error/11845339)). Mizuho sold 610,000 shares at one yen instead of one share at 610,000 yen, more than 41 times J-Com's outstanding shares ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2005/12/9/224m-typo-shakes-tokyo-exchange)).

The project's logs reproduce that split and show where plausibility acts. Price is the only concept where plausibility varies, because every non-US principal asks for 5 (distance) or 100 (speed). I split the eight non-US price clusters by whether the bare number is plausible as dollars. The implausible group is Korea (300,000), Japan (30,000) and India (15,000); the plausible group is France and the UK (300), Israel (800), Brazil (1,200) and China (1,500). The sender-side gradient is sharp. In the explicit arm, **Sonnet produced 0 of 18 silent errors on implausible budgets against 15 of 30 on plausible ones**, and Opus 0 of 18 against 7 of 30. The receiver-side gradient is weak for the headline models. In the counterfactual bare-number injection, **Sonnet and Haiku booked the budget in dollars in 9 of 9 implausible and 15 of 15 plausible items**, Opus in 8 of 9 against 15 of 15, and only Fable separated the groups (5 of 9 against 15 of 15). The proposed explanation, that receivers catch implausible readings, therefore fails for exactly the two models in the headline. The reformulation that fits both the literature and the logs is that **plausibility gates whichever agent first reads the bare number**, which under explicitness is the sender, and that it protects receivers only for some models. Even the sender's gate leaks under format pressure: in Sonnet's partial typed run, a sender filed India's 15,000 as `{"value": 15000, "unit": "USD"}`.

CAPRI's design cannot separate plausibility from currency. The three implausible clusters are exactly the three currencies far from the dollar, so plausibility is collinear with culture, exchange-rate distance and each culture's cue set. With eight clusters split three against five there are C(8,3) = 56 assignments. Even perfect separation therefore cannot pass a one-sided cluster permutation test below **p ≈ 0.018**, and any overlap leaves the claim untestable. The same structure inverts harm. The readings that slip through are the small-ratio ones: euro and pound budgets off by roughly 1.2–1.4×, and kilometres read as miles off by 1.6×. The roughly 1,400× won reading is the one that senders and some receivers catch. A severity-weighted silent-error rate, in the spirit of Bui et al.'s percentage-deviation reporting, would therefore rank conditions differently from the raw rate ([Bui et al. ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1032/)). A pre-committed value grid can break the collinearity only for speed. There the same principal can mean 30, 50, 100 or 130 km/h, and only the higher values become implausible as mph. Distance values could come from CAPRI's own file names (`cat-5m`, `hill-500m`, `apartment-5km`), but speed and price images carry none (`bus3.jpg`, `chair2.jpg`), so those values would have to come from an external published source fixed before any run. Price cannot vary plausibility within a currency, because a realistic budget inherits its currency's scale.

## Agent protocols type the payment but never the intent

The standards survey gives L4 both its motivation and its design vocabulary. A case-insensitive search of A2A 1.0's 812-line `a2a.proto` finds **zero** occurrences of "locale", "language", "currency", "timezone", "region", "country" or "i18n". Its input and output "modes" are MIME media types, and its only extension points are a free-form `Message.metadata` and an `AgentExtension` with a `required` flag ([a2a.proto](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/blob/main/specification/a2a.proto)). An open A2A proposal adds currency-tagged budgets, but for compute cost, not the user's purchase ([A2A #2121](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/issues/2121)). MCP tools declare JSON Schema inputs. The spec's own example puts the unit in a prose description ("Temperature in celsius"), while its unstructured result says "72°F" ([MCP tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/server/tools)). A proposal to add `clientContext.locale`, motivated by "dates, numbers, currency" and decimal separators, closed procedurally without merging. The live language proposal declares itself "language preference only, not a locale, timezone, currency, calendar, measurement" system ([SEP-1809](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/issues/1809); [SEP-2792](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/pull/2792)). OpenAPI 3.1 contains no occurrence of "unit", "currency" or "locale", and JSON Schema 2020-12 has no such keyword ([OpenAPI](https://spec.openapis.org/oas/v3.1.0.html); [JSON Schema](https://json-schema.org/draft/2020-12/json-schema-validation)). IBM's ACP carried `natural_languages` as ISO 639-1 codes before merging into A2A, which kept no equivalent ([ACP](https://github.com/i-am-bee/acp)). OpenAI's handoffs pass the full history by default. That carries the principal's context within one organisation, but across an A2A boundary only the message travels ([Agents SDK](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-python/handoffs/)). A 2026 survey of 18 protocols concludes that semantic responsibilities "are often pushed into prompts, wrappers, or application-specific orchestration logic" ([Yuan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02369)).

The symbolic agent languages had the slot, and their spec described the failure. FIPA ACL's `ontology` parameter "denotes the ontology(s) used to give a meaning to the symbols in the content expression", yet "may be omitted" when "commonly understood by the agent community". The same specification warns that implementations omitting parameters "are not guaranteed to interoperate with each other" ([FIPA SC00061G](http://www.fipa.org/specs/fipa00061/SC00061G.html)). KQML's canonical example fixes what "price" means with `:ontology NYSE-TICKS` ([KQML](https://research.cs.umbc.edu/kqml/papers/kqml-acl-html/section3.3.html)). LLM-era protocols dropped the slot when the content language became natural language, and FIPA's 2002 warning describes L4's failure condition.

Where money moves, the convention is typed. Google's AIP-141 requires the unit as a field-name suffix (`distance_km`). AIP-143 requires an ISO 4217 `currency_code`, `google.type.Money`, CLDR region codes and BCP 47 language codes ([AIP-141](https://google.aip.dev/141); [AIP-143](https://google.aip.dev/143)). L4's `radius_miles`, `mph` and `max_usd` therefore follow best practice, which makes the anchoring a finding about well-designed APIs rather than an artifact. Stripe requires a `currency` beside every `amount` in minor units ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/api/payment_intents/create)). AP2 shows L4's gap inside a single protocol. Its `IntentMandate` carries the user's intent only as `natural_language_description`, with **no amount or currency field**, while the generated payment mandate types `AmountRange` as an ISO 4217 code plus a maximum in minor units ([AP2 mandate.py](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/code/sdk/python/ap2/models/mandate.py); [AP2 open mandate](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/code/sdk/python/ap2/sdk/generated/open_payment_mandate.py)). Typing moves the convention rather than removing it. ISO 4217 assigns 0 minor digits to KRW and JPY, 2 to USD and EUR, and 3 to BHD, so 300,000 minor units is ₩300,000 or $3,000.00 ([ISO 4217 list](https://www.six-group.com/dam/download/financial-information/data-center/iso-currrency/lists/list-one.xml)). Stripe still represents ISK as two-decimal for backward compatibility ([Stripe currencies](https://docs.stripe.com/currencies)). For the principal's locale, BCP 47 with Unicode extensions already encodes measurement system (`-u-ms-`), unit override (`-u-mu-`), currency (`-u-cu-`) and region (`-u-rg-`). CLDR's unit preferences give kilometres for road distance worldwide but miles for the US and Great Britain ([UTS #35](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr35/); [CLDR preferences](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr35/tr35-info.html)).

| Design tested | Closest standard or precedent | What it implies for L4 |
|---|---|---|
| Sender states units | ISO 4217 code beside every amount (AIP-143, Stripe); FSF's rule that "all digits, as well as the unit" be announced; UCUM's goal of "unambiguous electronic communication of quantities together with their units" ([UCUM](https://ucum.org/ucum)) | Every standard makes the sender the carrier and assumes the sender knows the unit; L4's errors are the sender's own readings, which a free-text unit gives the receiver no way to audit |
| Locale card | No A2A field; `AgentExtension` (`required: true`) or `Message.metadata`; a BCP 47 tag such as `ko-KR-u-cu-krw` | A public card advertising a user's locale is a privacy exposure, so per-message metadata is the cleaner slot. Card fields helped routing only when the receiver reasoned with them (52% vs 32%, erased to 36% vs 36% by a keyword reader) ([Srinivasan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12213)), and Haiku's receivers let an explicit wrong unit override an oracle card |
| Receiver resolver | CLDR unit-preference data; FIPA `not-understood`; A2A `TASK_STATE_INPUT_REQUIRED` | Road distance and speed can be resolved deterministically from a region, but no standard settles a budget's currency, and CLDR and CAPRI's key disagree in places (the key accepts kilometres for UK distance, while CLDR prefers miles for GB roads). Without a principal, the resolver's "if unknown, ask" clause stalls |
| Typed JSON with a mandatory unit | `google.type.Money`; schema.org `MonetaryAmount` and `QuantitativeValue`, where `unitCode` is optional ([schema.org](https://schema.org/QuantitativeValue)); AP2 `AmountRange`; Agents SDK `input_type` | A schema guarantees that the field is present, not that it is true. Mars Climate Orbiter's interface spec "defines both the format and units" and "was not followed". Sonnet's partial typed run filled bare requests with `USD` or `mi` (48% silent wrong on 31 items), while Opus reached 2%. In Facts Without Rules, a corrupted typed allowlist brought leakage back near its unprotected level ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29028)) |
| Hybrid confidence | A2A `TASK_STATE_INPUT_REQUIRED`; ReConcile's confidence-bearing messages (+11.4%) ([ReConcile](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13007)) | The ask-back state already exists. The risks are calibration, since agents' self-confidence did not consistently beat a simple baseline ([Lin et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07395)), and stalls: Opus's hybrid has 1% silent wrong but 32% unresolved |
| Not tested: read-back | ICAO Annex 11 read-back and hearback; the rule to read back the meaning, not the abbreviation | An agent-to-agent read-back catches only receiver misdecoding; the sender's misreading needs a read-back to the principal |

The human safety domains name the design L4 lacks and show which half of the error it fixes. ICAO Annex 11 requires flight crews to read back safety-related clearances and controllers to check that read-back (the "hearback"). It lists "expectation bias" among the causes of failure, which is the human term for a receiver decoding by default ([ICAO IP/06](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/APAC/Meetings/2025/2025%20ATMSG13/04-Information%20Papers/IP06%20Importance%20of%20ATC%20Readback%20and%20Hearback%20%20.pdf)). Joint Commission guidance asks the recipient of a verbal order to read back the meaning of an abbreviation, not the abbreviation itself ([Joint Commission](https://www.jointcommission.org/en-us/knowledge-library/support-center/standards-interpretation/do-not-use-list-of-abbreviations)). These loops work because the party confirming the read-back, the controller or the prescriber, knows the true value. In L4 that party is the principal. An agent-to-agent read-back ("recording a maximum of 300 USD") catches a receiver's misdecoding of a bare number. It cannot catch the sender's own misreading, because the sender will confirm the unit it already believes. The standards-grounded channel the paper can recommend has five parts: a BCP 47 principal tag in per-message metadata, a typed `{value, unit}` or `google.type.Money` quantity, the sender's confidence, `INPUT_REQUIRED` when that confidence is low, and a read-back to the principal before irreversible actions.

## A reachable, gated principal makes ask versus act comparable

The current "safe = correct or asked" metric prices every question at zero, and the harness gives no agent anyone who can answer. The sender cannot reach its principal after the request, so its doubt either becomes an assertion or goes to a receiver that cannot resolve it. The result is stalls (80% of Sonnet's resolver episodes) and wrong actions after a question (8% of Opus's). The logs also keep only the sender's first message, truncated to 400 characters, so whether questions concern units or booking details cannot be audited. The four concurrent papers Review 3 cited share the cost blind spot. Matsnev's oracle reveals the full goal on any question. Edwards and Schuster cap interaction at three turns without charging for it, while Claude asks on 62–72% of tasks. Deng et al. report steps but optimise success, and Gulati et al. charge nothing ([Matsnev](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19559); [Edwards & Schuster](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26233); [Deng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03135); [Gulati et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07937)). Several 2026 papers argue that free asking inflates success. Ask When It Pays introduces a Weighted Success Rate that "penalizes each query by its derived cost" ([Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03175)). RegretBench scores regret against a reference clarification policy ([RegretBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21143)). Budget-constrained training finds untuned clarification "Pareto-inefficient" ([Rajput & Vogelstein](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04089)).

The literature's simulator design transfers directly, and it can be built so that the data rule holds. Privileged-information users descend from CLAM, which gave an LM hidden facts so that multi-turn clarification could be scored automatically ([CLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07769)), and from τ-bench, which scores the final database state and adds pass^k reliability ([τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)). Gating stops vague questions from being rewarded. NoisyToolBench's ToolEvaluator answers only when a question's embedding similarity to the expected question passes a threshold and otherwise replies "Sorry, I cannot provide additional information about this"; it agreed with human labels on 90% of 50 cases ([NoisyToolBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00557v4)). Deng et al.'s strict mode "reveals task-specific information only when the question is specific and relevant" ([Deng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03135)). Gating matters because prompted models "either ask for clarification on every query or ask vague questions" ([CIGAsk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24290)). For L4, the principal should know exactly two things: the unit or currency CAPRI's key assigns its culture, and the value already in the template. It answers only questions that target that quantity's unit, matched deterministically against the unit strings CAPRI's key already lists per culture. Every other question gets one fixed, pre-registered deferral that lets the episode proceed, so the simulator invents no facts. The principal must be reachable from both sides. The sender can ask before writing, which the logs show senders try to do, and the receiver's questions route to the sender, which answers from history or escalates. The two hops should be logged separately, because agent turns are cheap and human interrupts are not; CollabLLM reports a 10.4% cut in user time as an outcome in its own right ([CollabLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00640)). Episodes must run until an action or an explicit abstention, store full dialogues, and have the gate validated on a hand-checked sample, as ToolEvaluator was.

Scoring should report six outcomes instead of a binary "safe": correct without a question, silent wrong, asked then correct, asked then wrong, an unnecessary question, and never acted. Unnecessary questions show up on the same-convention controls the project already runs, and on items whose message already carries the right unit. They can be scored as clarification precision and recall against a label ("the message lacks the principal's unit or states a wrong one"), as Matsnev and Ambig-SWE do ([Ambig-SWE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13069)), with each question tagged necessary or unnecessary as in SovereignPA-Bench, where more faithful models asked fewer unnecessary questions (ρ = −0.67) ([SovereignPA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05363)). ClarifyCodeBench shows why detection, not resolution, is the bottleneck: once units are visibly ambiguous in a code spec, they are among the best-resolved types, at 66.7% for three models ([ClarifyCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00711)). The headline comparison then becomes a utility, `U = 1[correct] − λ_a·(agent questions) − λ_p·(principal escalations) − λ_w·1[wrong action]`. It should be swept over a log grid, which makes Tsvilodub et al.'s expected-regret rule explicit. Alongside it go regret against an oracle policy (ask if and only if the unit is missing or wrong) and a frontier of correct and silent-error rates against question rate. The frontier comes from sweeping a graded hybrid threshold, with always-ask, never-ask and a Matsnev-style receiver self-uncertainty baseline as reference points, plus comparisons at matched question budgets ([Zhang & Choi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09469)). A conformal threshold in the style of KnowNo then gives the question rate needed to guarantee, for example, at most 1% silent errors ([KnowNo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928)).

A first pass on the current logs shows why the rerun must precede the headline. I treated each episode with a question as one question, set λ in units of one successful completion, and compared arms pairwise.

| Comparison on current logs | Arm A beats arm B only if |
|---|---|
| Sonnet: receiver resolver (A) vs sender explicit (B) | `λ_w > 2.3 + 2.9·λ_a` |
| Opus: receiver resolver vs sender explicit | `λ_w > 6.2 + 7.3·λ_a`; never, if wrong actions after a question also count (10.7% vs 10.1%) |
| Opus: hybrid vs the same protocol without confidence | `λ_w > 2.8 + 3.4·λ_a` |
| Opus: typed vs free-text explicit | `λ_w > 0.17 + 0.96·λ_a` |
| Haiku: oracle card + resolver vs the same plus sender explicitness | At every λ (A dominates) |

Even with free questions, Sonnet's resolver beats explicitness only if one silent error costs more than 2.3 successful completions, and Opus's only above 6.2. The typed message beats free-text explicitness at almost any weight. These crossovers will move once a principal answers, because stalled arms will convert questions into correct actions. That is the whole case for running the end-to-end protocol before fixing the headline. Intervals should be bootstrapped over the 28 clusters, with paired exact tests on shared items.

Several literature-suggested ideas fail the constraints or the budget and should be cut or deferred. The receiver-locale swap cannot touch first messages. Number-format perturbations such as decimal commas, lakh and 万 ([WorldBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01056v1)), and templates placing an Iranian user on a US road trip, would require self-built inputs. The hand-built "Lost in Convention" false-friend cases are outside the paper and cannot be cited as support. CAPRI's cue ladder is unavailable. The release in the repo holds exactly the 4,992 full implicit and 516 neutral conversations, and the intermediate cue levels and the ExplicitFull variant would have to be written by hand; only the neutral contrast in rank 6 survives. Latent-channel arms, base-versus-instruct comparisons, activation probes and a human-principal study are worthwhile but not cheap. Typed variants are consistent with the rule but lower in value this cycle: `google.type.Money` or Stripe-style minor units (KRW and JPY at 0 decimals against USD and EUR at 2), and declared against validated unit fields. The queued cross-provider pairs are best reported as a role-reversal matrix, since reversing which provider sells moved negotiation outcomes by 7–18 points elsewhere ([Liang & Xu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07538)).

## Conclusion

The literature recasts "which side should carry the convention" as a question about who can be asked. Standards from FIPA to ISO 4217, and safety practice in aviation and medicine, put the unit on the sender, and the logs confirm that receivers handle a stated unit well, with no silent errors when it is right. What breaks is the sender's freedom to doubt. The explicit instruction turns a sender that writes "I don't want to guess" into one that guesses in dollars and miles, and no agent in the harness can reach the principal who knows the answer. That headline is sharper and more defensible than an accuracy–safety trade-off. It has no direct precedent, it ties CAPRI's gap, the uncertainty-expression literature and the protocol gap into one mechanism, and it can be tested with prompt changes alone.

For the paper, the practical lesson is that the current safety numbers measure stalls and that plausibility belongs to the first reader, not to the receiver. Once a principal answers and every question has a price, the comparison the theme track rewards becomes possible: which channel routes doubt to the person who holds the convention, at the lowest cost. Today's protocols leave out exactly that channel, which would carry a BCP 47 tag, a typed quantity, a confidence and an input-required state. L4 can be the first paper to measure what it buys.
